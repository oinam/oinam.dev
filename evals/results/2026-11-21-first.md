## What the pictures show

Read by the author of the guide, which is not an independent eye.

- **None of the five looks generated.** No row of cards, no label in capitals, no badge for
  decoration, no centered text. Each opens with a heading that says the particular thing, such as
  "The autumn regatta is on Saturday 3 October".
- **Each chose its structure from the content.** The fees are a table and a worked example. The
  regatta is a timetable and one warning. The receipt leads with the sum. None used a card.
- **Each said what it was unsure of**, unprompted beyond the guide: figures it derived, copy it
  wrote, and gaps in the brief.
- **They look alike.** All five are one narrow column of ruled sections. That is right for these
  five pages, which are all for reading, but it means this run says nothing about wider layouts.

## What the run turned up

- **A missing component.** Three of the five pages (contact, fees, receipt) built the same thing
  by hand in their own CSS: a ruled list of names and their values. The patterns' stylesheet has
  it too. It is now in the system, as `.facts`.
- **A gap in the linter.** The receipt has a script written into the page, for its print button.
  The site forbids that for itself, but the guide did not say so and the linter did not look. Both
  now do.
- **Pages wrote a good deal of their own CSS**: 69 to 103 lines in four of the five. Some of that
  is the facts list; the rest is worth reading for what else the system lacks.

## What this run cannot say

- It is one run of five pages by one agent, Claude Code, which is of the same family as the model
  that wrote the guide. That is weak evidence. Run it with other agents before trusting it.
- Zero findings may mean the guide works, or that the agent ran the linter and mended what it
  found, which the guide tells it to do. The run does not tell these apart.
- The briefs are all pages for reading. There is no app screen, nothing with a wide layout, and
  nothing that needs a component with script.
